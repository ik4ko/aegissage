Usually Part B. Part B covers most drugs you would not give to yourself, like infusions and injections you get in a doctor's office, an infusion center or a hospital outpatient department. Drugs you take on your own at home are usually covered by a Part D drug plan.

## Why does it matter which part covers it?

Because it changes what you pay and what you need to check:

- **Original Medicare:** you generally pay 20% of the Medicare-approved amount after the Part B deductible. A Medigap policy can cover some or all of that.
- **Medicare Advantage:** the plan must cover the same infusion, but your share depends on the plan. The plan may require prior authorization, and you may need an in-network infusion center.
- **Part D:** only comes in for drugs you get at a pharmacy and take yourself.

## What about infusions at home?

Medicare covers certain drugs infused at home through Part B-covered equipment, such as an infusion pump, when they are medically necessary. Ask your doctor's office how your specific drug is billed.

## Questions to ask before you choose a plan

1. Where do I get my infusion, and is that location in the plan's network?
2. Does the plan require prior authorization for it?
3. What is my share per infusion, and what is the plan's yearly out-of-pocket limit?
4. If I use a patient assistance grant, does it still apply with this coverage?
