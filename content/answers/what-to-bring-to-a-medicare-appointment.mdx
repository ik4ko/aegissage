Bring five things to a Medicare appointment: your Medicare card, a list of your prescriptions with doses, the names of your doctors and pharmacies, your current insurance cards, and any recent letters from your plan or your employer. That is enough for a useful conversation. You do not need to bring bank or credit card details to get information.

## What should I bring to a Medicare appointment?

Bring your Medicare card or the letter showing when it starts, every prescription with its exact name and dose, your doctors and pharmacy, and any letters from your current coverage. The card's dates show which enrollment window you are in, so bring it.

1. **Your Medicare card**, or the letter that says when your Medicare starts. The card shows whether you have Part A, Part B or both, and the date each began. Those dates decide which enrollment window you are in.
2. **Your prescriptions.** The exact name, the dose and how often you take each one. Plans cover different drugs, so this is checked drug by drug.
3. **Your doctors, hospital and pharmacy.** Names and towns. Medicare's advice before joining a plan is to ask your doctors and pharmacies whether they are in the plan's network.
4. **Your current coverage.** Insurance cards from an employer, a union, a retiree plan, the VA or Medicaid.
5. **Recent letters.** Your plan's Annual Notice of Change if you got one in the fall. A notice from your employer or plan saying whether your drug coverage is creditable. Anything that gives the date your coverage ends.

If you spend part of the year in another state, write that down too.

## What do I not need to bring to a Medicare appointment?

You do not need your bank account or credit card number. Medicare's rules do not let a plan or its agent ask for financial information just to give you a quote. You also do not need to share your Medicare number just to ask questions.

Medicare's rules do not allow a plan or its representative to ask for financial information just to give you a quote.

You also do not need to hand over your Medicare number to ask questions. It is needed only if and when you decide to join a plan. Medicare says to protect it the way you would a credit card. Please do not send it through a website form or an email.

## What does a Medicare agent have to do at the appointment?

Medicare sets rules. You agree to the meeting and fill out an appointment form first, and the agent can discuss only the kinds of products you agreed to. The agent must also tell you how many organizations and plans they represent where you live.

Medicare has rules for the meeting itself:

- You agree to the meeting and complete an appointment form first. The agent can talk only about the kinds of products you agreed to hear about.
- The agent must tell you how many organizations they represent and how many plans they offer in your area before discussing specific plan benefits.
- The agent cannot charge you a fee to enroll, pressure you to join, or ask you to sign an enrollment form before you are ready.

## What questions should I ask at a Medicare appointment?

Three worth asking: which plans do you not offer here, are all my drugs covered and at which pharmacy, and what changes if I travel or move? Write them down before the meeting. The printable appointment checklist has room for these and your notes.
