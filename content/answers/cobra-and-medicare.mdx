COBRA lets you keep an employer's group health plan for a limited time after a job ends, but it does not give you more time to sign up for Medicare Part B. You have up to 8 months after you stop working or lose the coverage from that job, whichever happens first, whether or not you choose COBRA. If you are 65 or older, sign up for Medicare first and treat COBRA as extra.

## What is COBRA?

COBRA is a federal law that lets you keep your employer's health coverage for a while after the job ends, usually for 18 months and sometimes 36. It generally applies to employers with 20 or more employees. Your former employer's benefits office can tell you what you are offered.

COBRA is a federal law. In general it applies to employers with 20 or more employees, and coverage is usually offered for 18 months, or 36 months in some cases. Some states have their own laws for smaller employers. Your employer's benefits administrator can tell you what you are offered and what it costs.

## Does COBRA give me more time to sign up for Part B?

No. Medicare's extra time to sign up for Part B is for coverage from a current job. COBRA is coverage from a former job, so your 8 months start when the work or the job coverage ends, and they keep running while you are on COBRA.

Medicare gives you a Special Enrollment Period to sign up for Part B after coverage from a **current** job ends. COBRA is coverage from a **former** job. So the 8 months start when the work or the coverage from that work ends, and they keep running while you are on COBRA.

If you miss the 8 months:

- You wait for the General Enrollment Period, January 1 to March 31.
- Coverage starts the month after you sign up, which can leave a gap.
- You may pay a Part B late enrollment penalty for as long as you have Part B.

## If I have COBRA and Medicare, which one pays first?

It depends on which one you had first. Medicare usually pays first once you have it, and COBRA pays second, but the details turn on the order you got them in. The section below explains each case, so check it before you rely on COBRA.

It depends on the order you got them.

- **You have COBRA and then become eligible for Medicare.** Your COBRA will probably end once you sign up for Medicare. Until you sign up, COBRA may pay only a small portion of your bills.
- **You have Medicare and then get COBRA.** Medicare pays first and COBRA pays after it. The rule is different for people who have Medicare because of End-Stage Renal Disease.

## Do I need a Medicare drug plan if I have COBRA?

Only if the COBRA drug coverage is not creditable. Ask the COBRA plan. If it is creditable, you can wait and join a Medicare drug plan when COBRA ends. If it is not, waiting can lead to a Part D late enrollment penalty that lasts.

Ask the COBRA plan whether its drug coverage is creditable. If it is, you can wait to join a Medicare drug plan, and you get a window to join one when COBRA ends. If it is not, waiting can lead to a Part D late enrollment penalty.
