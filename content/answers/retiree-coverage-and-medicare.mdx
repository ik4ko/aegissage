Retiree coverage from a former employer or union works with Medicare, not in place of it. Medicare generally pays first and the retiree plan pays second. You may need both Part A and Part B to get full benefits from the retiree plan. Check with the plan before you join any Medicare plan, because joining one can end the retiree coverage.

## Retiree coverage is not coverage from a current job

This is the point that matters most for timing. The rule that lets people delay Part B without a penalty applies to group health coverage from **current** work. Retiree coverage comes from a former job. If retiree coverage is all you have, Medicare says to sign up when you turn 65, during your Initial Enrollment Period.

Medicare also says retiree coverage might not pay your medical costs for any time you were eligible for Medicare but had not signed up.

## Three questions for your benefits administrator

1. Will my benefits, or my family's, change when I become eligible for Medicare?
2. Is the drug coverage creditable?
3. Does the employer offer its own coverage for people with Medicare, such as a supplemental plan, drug coverage or a Medicare Advantage Plan? If I join a plan the employer does not offer, do I or my family lose the retiree coverage?

Ask for the answers in writing, and ask for the plan's benefit booklet.

## Drug coverage

You can join a Medicare drug plan when you sign up for Part A or Part B. If you do not, you need creditable drug coverage to avoid a Part D late enrollment penalty. Your retiree plan has to tell you each year whether its drug coverage is creditable. Keep that notice.

If your income and resources are limited, you may still be able to get Extra Help even with creditable retiree drug coverage. Talk to your benefits administrator before you change anything.

## Medigap

Because Medicare pays first after you retire, retiree coverage often does a job similar to a Medigap policy. That is a reason some people do not buy one. The cost of that choice is timing. Your Medigap Open Enrollment Period lasts 6 months from when you first have Part B at 65 or older. It does not come back if the retiree coverage later ends or changes. See [Medigap in New Jersey](/answers/medigap-new-jersey).
