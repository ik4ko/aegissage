How you check depends on the kind of coverage. With Original Medicare, ask the doctor's office two things: do you take Medicare, and do you accept assignment? With a Medicare Advantage Plan, look the doctor up in the plan's provider directory and then confirm by phone with the doctor's office, using the plan's exact name.

## If you have Original Medicare

You can use any doctor or hospital in the country that takes Medicare. There is no network. What changes your cost is assignment.

- A provider who **accepts assignment** takes the Medicare-approved amount as full payment. You pay the deductible and coinsurance, and they send the claim to Medicare.
- A provider who **does not accept assignment** can charge more than the Medicare-approved amount, within a limit, and may ask you to pay in full at the visit.
- A provider who has **opted out** of Medicare is not paid by Medicare at all, except in an emergency.

You can look a doctor up with Medicare's Care Compare tool to find out whether they accept assignment or participate in Medicare.

## If you have, or are looking at, a Medicare Advantage Plan

Plans have networks, and the plan type sets the rules:

- **HMO.** In general you must use doctors and hospitals in the network. The exceptions are emergency care, urgent care when you are out of the area, and temporary dialysis out of the area.
- **PPO.** You pay less in the network. You can generally go outside it for covered services, but you will usually pay more.

### Five steps

1. Get the plan's full name, including the letters HMO or PPO. One company can offer several plans with different networks.
2. Search the plan's provider directory for each doctor.
3. Call each doctor's office. Ask: "Are you in the network for this plan next year?" and read them the full name.
4. Do the same for your hospital and for any specialist you see regularly. A doctor can be in a network when the hospital they use is not.
5. Check your pharmacy. Some plans cover drugs only at in-network pharmacies, and some have preferred pharmacies that cost you less.

Write down who you spoke to and the date.

## Check again every fall

Networks and contracts change from year to year. Your plan's Annual Notice of Change arrives in September. Open Enrollment, October 15 to December 7, is when you can change plans for January 1. If you live near a state line and see doctors in New York while living in New Jersey, check each one. Plans are offered by county, and a plan offered where you live may or may not include doctors across the state line.

## If your doctor leaves the network

Call the plan and ask what your options are. In some cases Medicare allows a Special Enrollment Period after a significant network change.
