Every Medicare Advantage plan has a yearly limit on what you pay for services covered by Part A and Part B. Once you reach it, the plan pays for covered services for the rest of the year. A PPO usually shows two limits: one for in-network care, and a higher one for in-network and out-of-network care combined.

## How do the two limits work?

- **In-network limit.** Your copays and coinsurance for in-network care add up toward this cap. Once you reach it, in-network care is covered for the rest of the year.
- **Combined limit.** Everything counts toward this one: in-network costs and out-of-network costs together. It is the higher number, and it is your worst case if you also use doctors outside the network.

Seeing one out-of-network doctor does **not** move you onto the higher limit. In-network costs count toward both caps; out-of-network costs count only toward the combined one.

## What doesn't count?

- Your monthly premiums, including the Part B premium.
- Part D drug costs, which follow their own yearly rules.
- Extra benefits outside Part A and Part B, such as some dental or vision coverage, depending on the plan.

## Where do I find my plan's numbers?

In the plan's Summary of Benefits, and in the Annual Notice of Change your plan mails each fall. Both limits can change from year to year, so check them each year before December 7.
