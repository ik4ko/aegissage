You can change Medicare Advantage or drug coverage every year from October 15 to December 7. Outside those dates you need one of the other enrollment periods below. Each one allows different changes, so the first question is always which window you are in.

## Signing up for Medicare itself (Part A and Part B)

- **Initial Enrollment Period.** Seven months around your 65th birthday: 3 months before the month you turn 65, that month, and 3 months after.
- **Special Enrollment Period for people with coverage from current work.** Any time while that coverage continues, and up to 8 months after the job or the coverage ends, whichever comes first.
- **General Enrollment Period.** January 1 to March 31 each year, for people who missed the windows above. Coverage starts the month after you sign up, and a late enrollment penalty may apply.

## Joining or changing a Medicare Advantage or drug plan

- **When you are new to Medicare.** Your first chance follows the same 7-month window.
- **Open Enrollment, October 15 to December 7.** Join, switch or drop a Medicare Advantage or drug plan, or move between Original Medicare and Medicare Advantage. Changes start January 1.
- **Medicare Advantage Open Enrollment, January 1 to March 31.** Only for people already in a Medicare Advantage plan. You can switch to another Medicare Advantage plan or return to Original Medicare and join a drug plan, once.
- **Special Enrollment Periods.** Tied to events in your life: you move, you lose other coverage, your plan's contract with Medicare ends, you become eligible for Medicaid or Extra Help, and others. Each has its own dates.

## The 5-star Special Enrollment Period

Medicare rates plans from 1 to 5 stars for quality and performance. If a plan with an overall rating of 5 stars is available in your area, you can use this Special Enrollment Period to switch to it **one time between December 8 and November 30** of the plan year.

Three things to know before using it:

- It exists only where a 5-star plan is offered. Ratings are published on medicare.gov and can change from one year to the next.
- You must still meet that plan's requirements, including living in its service area.
- If you move from a plan that includes drug coverage to a 5-star plan that does not, you can lose drug coverage and may have to wait for the next enrollment period to get it back.

A high rating describes the plan overall. It does not tell you whether your doctors are in its network or how your prescriptions are covered. Check those first.

## Medigap is on a different clock

A Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policy has its own one-time 6-month window that starts when you are 65 or older and have Part B. It does not reopen every fall. See [Medigap in New Jersey](/answers/medigap-new-jersey).
