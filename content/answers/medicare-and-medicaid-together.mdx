You can have Medicare and Medicaid at the same time. When you do, Medicare pays first for the services it covers and Medicaid pays last. Medicaid can also cover things Medicare normally does not, such as nursing home care and personal care services. Whether a person can get Medicaid is decided by their state, under that state's rules.

## What Medicaid adds when you have Medicare

For people with Medicare and full-benefit Medicaid, Medicare says:

- The state pays the monthly Part B premium.
- Depending on the level of Medicaid, the state might also pay your share of Medicare costs, such as deductibles, coinsurance and copayments, and the Part A premium if you have one.
- You get Extra Help with your drug costs automatically.
- Medicaid may pay for some drugs and services Medicare does not cover.

## Prescriptions

Medicare covers your prescription drugs, not Medicaid. You are enrolled in a Medicare drug plan, and Extra Help lowers what you pay.

## You still choose how to get Medicare

You can stay in Original Medicare or join a Medicare Advantage Plan. Some Medicare Advantage Plans are built for people with both programs:

- **Dual Eligible Special Needs Plans.** A kind of Special Needs Plan for people with Medicare and Medicaid. Some are "integrated", which means the plan helps coordinate the two sets of benefits. Not every one is.
- **PACE**, the Program of All-inclusive Care for the Elderly, which helps certain people get care outside a nursing home.

These are offered in some places and not others. This page describes how they work in general. It does not say that any plan is available to you or right for you.

## Changing plans

Most people can change plans only at set times of year. People who have Medicaid or get Extra Help can switch drug plans, or leave a Medicare Advantage Plan with drug coverage for Original Medicare and a separate drug plan, once a calendar month. If you stop being eligible for Medicaid, you have 3 full months to change plans.

## If your income is a little too high for Medicaid

Medicare Savings Programs are separate, state-run programs that help pay Part A and Part B premiums and, in some cases, other costs. In the Qualified Medicare Beneficiary program, Medicare providers are not allowed to bill you for services Medicare covers. Medicare's advice is to apply even if you are not sure, because states count income and resources differently.

## Where to apply

Medicaid and Medicare Savings Programs are applied for through your state, and you must live there.

- **New Jersey:** the NJSave application covers the Medicare Savings Programs and the state's prescription programs. See [help paying Medicare costs in New Jersey](/answers/extra-help-medicare-savings-programs-new-jersey).
- **New York:** the New York State Department of Health runs Medicaid. See [Medicare help in New York City](/answers/medicare-help-new-york-city).
- **Pennsylvania:** the Department of Human Services runs Medicaid.

Your State Health Insurance Assistance Program can help with the forms at no cost. For plan changes, see [how to switch Medicare plans](/answers/how-to-switch-medicare-plans).
