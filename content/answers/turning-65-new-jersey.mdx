If you are turning 65 in New Jersey, you have a 7-month window to sign up for Medicare: it opens 3 months before the month of your 65th birthday and closes 3 months after it. Do three things in order: find out whether you are enrolled automatically, decide whether you need Part B now or can wait because of employer coverage, then choose how you want to receive your coverage.

## Step 1: find out whether you are enrolled automatically

People who are already receiving Social Security or Railroad Retirement benefits at least 4 months before they turn 65 are generally enrolled in Part A and Part B automatically. Everyone else has to sign up through Social Security. If you are not sure which group you are in, that is the first call to make.

## Step 2: decide about Part B

If you or your spouse are still working and you are covered by that employer's group health plan, you may be able to delay Part B without a penalty. Retiree coverage and COBRA do not count as coverage from current work. I wrote a separate page about this because it is the most expensive thing to get wrong: [leaving employer coverage after 65](/answers/leaving-employer-coverage-after-65). There is also a longer guide on [working past 65](/medicare-basics/still-working-at-65).

If you do not have coverage from current work, missing your window can mean waiting for the General Enrollment Period (January 1 to March 31) and paying a late enrollment penalty for as long as you have Part B.

## Step 3: choose how you get your coverage

Once you have Part A and Part B there are two main routes:

- **Original Medicare**, usually with a separate drug plan (Part D) and, if you want one, a Medicare Supplement (Medigap) policy.
- **A Medicare Advantage plan (Part C)**, a private plan that combines hospital and medical coverage and often drug coverage.

Which one fits depends on your doctors, your prescriptions and your budget. In New Jersey it also depends on your county, because Medicare Advantage and drug plans are offered county by county. A plan available in Bergen County is not necessarily available in Hudson County.

One timing point is specific to Medigap: your Medigap Open Enrollment Period lasts 6 months, starts the first month you are 65 or older and have Part B, and does not repeat. See [Medigap in New Jersey](/answers/medigap-new-jersey).

## Where New Jersey residents can get help at no cost

- **NJ SHIP** counselors explain Medicare and do not sell insurance: 1-800-792-8820.
- **NJSave** is one application for New Jersey's programs that help with Medicare premiums and prescription costs. See [help paying Medicare costs in New Jersey](/answers/extra-help-medicare-savings-programs-new-jersey).
- The [enrollment window tool](/tools/eligibility-check) on this site tells you which window you are in.

If you live in or near Edgewater, Fort Lee or Cliffside Park, the [Bergen County page](/medicare-bergen-county) explains why the county line matters more than the town line.
