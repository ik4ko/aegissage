Medical underwriting means an insurance company looks at your health before it decides whether to sell you a Medigap policy and at what price. In New Jersey, the federal rules apply. A company cannot turn you down for health reasons during your one-time 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period. It also cannot turn you down when you have a guaranteed issue right. New York goes further and requires insurers to accept a Medigap application at any time of year.

## What is the federal Medigap open enrollment rule?

Your Medigap Open Enrollment Period lasts 6 months and starts the first month you have Part B and are 65 or older. During it, you can buy any Medigap policy sold in your state, and the company cannot deny you because of health problems.

After it ends, Medicare says you may not be able to buy a policy, or it may cost more. The exceptions are guaranteed issue rights: specific situations, each with a deadline, in which a company must sell you certain policies. Here are two common ones. Your Medicare Advantage Plan leaves Medicare or stops covering where you live. Or you joined a Medicare Advantage Plan when you were first eligible at 65, and you switch back to Original Medicare within the first year.

Medicare says these answers cover federal rights only, and tells you to check with your state for more.

## What are the Medigap rules in New Jersey?

For people 65 and older, New Jersey uses the federal 6-month window. What the state adds is for people under 65 with Medicare because of a disability: insurers that sell Medigap to people 65 and older must also offer a plan to them.

For people 65 and older, the 6-month period above is the window in New Jersey. What the state adds, by law, is for people under 65 who have Medicare because of a disability:

- Insurers that sell Medicare Supplement plans to people 65 and older must also offer a plan to people who become eligible for Medicare from age 50 through 64.
- A separate state program covers people under 50.
- In both, a person who became eligible on or after January 1, 2020 must apply within 12 months after enrolling in Part B.
- The price cannot be more than the company charges a 65-year-old for the same policy.

The details are in [Medigap in New Jersey](/answers/medigap-new-jersey).

## What are the Medigap rules in New York?

New York law requires any insurer that sells Medigap to accept an application from a person with Medicare at any time of year. Insurers may not deny the policy or charge more because of health, claims history or medical condition.

New York State law requires any insurer writing Medigap insurance to accept a Medicare enrollee's application at any time throughout the year. Insurers may not deny the policy or set a different premium because of health status, claims history or medical condition. The state's counseling program describes New York premiums as community-rated: they vary by where you live and by company, not by your age or health.

One thing still applies. A New York policy may have a waiting period of up to 6 months before it covers a pre-existing condition. Insurers must shorten it by the time you were covered before, as long as you had no break in coverage of more than 63 days.

These protections cover people who have Medicare because of age or disability.
