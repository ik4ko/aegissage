If you delayed Medicare Part B because you had health coverage from your own or your spouse's current job, you can sign up without a late penalty while that coverage continues, or for up to 8 months after the job or the coverage ends, whichever happens first. To avoid a gap, sign up the month before the coverage ends.

## The rule, in Medicare's words

Medicare calls this a Special Enrollment Period. It applies when the coverage came from **current** employment, yours or your spouse's. It starts once your Initial Enrollment Period is over and it ends 8 months after the group health plan coverage or the employment ends, whichever comes first.

## Three things that catch people

- **COBRA does not count.** Choosing COBRA does not move the end of the 8-month window. People who rely on COBRA for a year and then try to enroll find that the window has closed.
- **Retiree coverage does not count either.** It is not coverage from current work.
- **Missing the window has two costs.** You may have to wait for the General Enrollment Period (January 1 to March 31), and you may pay a late enrollment penalty for as long as you have Part B.

## What to do, in order

1. Ask your employer's benefits office for the date your group coverage ends, in writing.
2. Sign up for Part B through Social Security. If you already have Part A you can apply for Part B online. You will need a form from your employer showing you had coverage from current work.
3. Decide how you want your Medicare coverage: Original Medicare with a drug plan and possibly Medigap, or a Medicare Advantage plan. Getting Part B opens a limited window to join a Medicare Advantage or drug plan, and it starts your one-time 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period.

## If your employer is small

Whether your employer plan or Medicare pays first can depend on the size of the employer. That is a separate question from the enrollment window and it matters before you decide to delay. The guide on [working past 65](/medicare-basics/still-working-at-65) covers it.
