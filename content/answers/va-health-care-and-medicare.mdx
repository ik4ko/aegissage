You can have VA health care and Medicare at the same time, and the VA encourages veterans to sign up for Medicare at 65. The two programs do not pay for the same service. Each time you get care, you choose which benefit to use: Medicare pays for Medicare-covered care, and the VA pays for care it provides or authorizes.

## How the two fit together

- **Medicare** pays for Medicare-covered items and services from providers that take Medicare.
- **The VA** pays for VA-authorized items and services, in a VA facility or in a non-VA facility the VA has approved ahead of time.
- If the VA authorizes some of the services in a non-VA hospital stay but not all of them, Medicare may pay for the Medicare-covered services the VA did not authorize.

The VA does not bill Medicare. You do not lose VA health care by having other insurance; the VA says your insurance status does not affect the VA benefits you can get.

## Why the VA suggests signing up for Medicare

The VA gives these reasons on its own website:

1. With Medicare you are covered if you need to go to a non-VA hospital or doctor, so you have more choices.
2. Funding for VA health care could change in the future. Veterans in lower priority groups could lose VA health care benefits.
3. If you delay Part B and sign up later, you will pay a late enrollment penalty. It gets bigger each year you waited, and you pay it for as long as you have Part B.

The VA also notes that if you have Part B and cancel it, you cannot get it back until January of the following year, and you may pay a penalty.

## Part B timing

VA health care is not coverage from a current job. The window that lets people delay Part B without a penalty is for people who have group health coverage from their own or a spouse's current work. If you are not in that situation, your time to sign up for Part B without a penalty is your 7-month Initial Enrollment Period around your 65th birthday. See [still working at 65](/answers/medicare-while-still-working) if you have both a job plan and VA care.

## Drug coverage

Medicare counts VA drug coverage as creditable drug coverage. That means you can wait on Part D without a penalty while you have it, and you have 63 days to enroll if the VA coverage ends. Some veterans add a Medicare drug plan so they can fill prescriptions from non-VA doctors at a local pharmacy. You cannot use both for the same drug at the same time.

## What to do next

Keep your VA doctor informed about any care you get outside the VA. For the dates, see the [enrollment calendar](/medicare-enrollment-calendar) and [turning 65 in New Jersey](/answers/turning-65-new-jersey).
