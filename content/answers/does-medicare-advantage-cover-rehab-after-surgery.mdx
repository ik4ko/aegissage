Yes. Medicare Advantage plans must cover all medically necessary services that Original Medicare covers, so rehab after surgery is covered, including inpatient rehabilitation, skilled nursing facility care and home health care when you meet the conditions. What differs from plan to plan is your share of the cost, which facilities are in the network, and whether the plan needs to approve the care first.

## What to check in a plan before you need it

- **Daily copays** for a skilled nursing facility and for inpatient rehab. These are often charged per day, so look at how they add up over a few weeks.
- **The yearly out-of-pocket limit.** It caps what you pay for Part A and Part B services in a year.
- **Prior authorization.** Many plans need to approve rehab stays and some therapy before they pay.
- **The network.** Check whether the rehab facilities near you, and near your family, are in the plan's network.

## How is this different from Original Medicare?

With Original Medicare you can use any facility that takes Medicare, and in most cases you do not need prior authorization. You pay Medicare's deductibles and coinsurance, and a Medigap policy can cover much of that.

## What Medicare does not cover

Neither Original Medicare nor Medicare Advantage pays for long-term custodial care, the everyday help with bathing, dressing or eating. Medicare covers skilled care for a limited time while you recover.
