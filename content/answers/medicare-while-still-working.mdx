If you or your spouse are still working when you turn 65 and you have group health coverage from that job, you can usually wait to sign up for Medicare Part B without a late enrollment penalty. When the work or the coverage ends, you get 8 months to sign up. Before you decide to wait, find out whether the employer's plan or Medicare pays first.

## If I work past 65, does my job's plan or Medicare pay first?

It depends on the employer's size. With 20 or more employees, the job's group health plan pays first and Medicare pays second. With fewer than 20, Medicare pays first. That matters, because a plan that pays second may pay very little.

Medicare's rule for people 65 or older with coverage from a current job turns on one number:

- **20 or more employees.** The group health plan pays first and Medicare pays second.
- **Fewer than 20 employees.** Medicare pays first and the group health plan pays second.

This matters because a plan that pays second may pay little or nothing for a service if you do not have Medicare. Medicare's own advice is to ask the employer that provides your coverage whether you need Part A and Part B when you turn 65.

## What counts as coverage from a current job?

Group health insurance from your own or your spouse's current work, offered to everyone at the company. It does not include retiree coverage or COBRA, which come from a former job. If you are self-employed, Medicare says to sign up at 65 to avoid a penalty.

The rule covers group health insurance from your own or your spouse's current work that is open to everyone at the company. If you are self-employed, or your coverage is not an employer group health plan, Medicare says to sign up at 65 to avoid a Part B late enrollment penalty.

## Should I sign up for Part A if I have a Health Savings Account?

Check first. If Part A is premium-free, you can sign up at 65 or later. But if you contribute to a Health Savings Account, Medicare says you and your employer should stop contributing 6 months before you retire or apply for Social Security.

If you do not have to pay a premium for Part A, you can sign up for it at 65 or at any time later. There is one thing to check first. Part A can start up to 6 months back from when you sign up, and contributions made while you have Medicare can lead to a tax penalty.

## Do I need a Medicare drug plan while I am still working?

Not if your current drug coverage is creditable. Your plan must tell you each year whether it is, so keep that letter. You will not owe a Part D late penalty as long as you never go 63 days or more without creditable drug coverage.

You can wait to join a Medicare drug plan as long as your current drug coverage is creditable. Keep that letter.

## What should I do when my job is ending?

Get the date your group coverage ends in writing. Sign up for Part B about a month before that date so there is no gap. You have 8 months from the end of the work or the coverage, whichever comes first, and COBRA does not add time.

1. Find out the date your group coverage ends, in writing.
2. Sign up for Part B about a month before that date so there is no gap. Medicare coverage starts the month after Social Security processes your form.
3. You have 8 months from the end of the work or the coverage, whichever comes first. Choosing COBRA does not add time.
4. Once Part B starts, your one-time 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period starts with it.
