Philadelphia residents have three places to start with Medicare. The first is PA MEDI, for free counseling that is not tied to any insurance company. The second is PACE and PACENET, for help with prescription costs if you are 65 or older and qualify. The third is the federal enrollment windows that apply everywhere. Medicare Advantage and drug plans are offered by county, and the City of Philadelphia is its own county.

## Where can I get free Medicare counseling in Philadelphia?

From PA MEDI, Pennsylvania's Medicare counseling program, run through local Area Agencies on Aging. Its counselors do not sell insurance or recommend any company, product or agent. They can help you compare coverage and understand your options at no cost.

PA MEDI (Pennsylvania Medicare Education and Decision Insight) is the state's Medicare counseling program, run through local Area Agencies on Aging. According to the Pennsylvania Department of Aging, its counselors do not sell insurance products. They do not endorse or recommend any insurance company, product or agent. They can compare plans with you and check whether you may be eligible for cost-savings programs.

Helpline: 1-800-783-7067, Monday to Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.

## What are PACE and PACENET in Pennsylvania?

They are Pennsylvania's prescription help programs for qualified residents 65 and older. They work with Medicare Part D plans and other drug coverage, such as retiree or Medicare Advantage coverage, to lower what you pay. Income and residency rules apply.

To apply you must be 65 or older and have lived in Pennsylvania for at least 90 days. Eligibility is based on your income for the previous calendar year, and the limits are set by the state. The program's phone number is 1-800-225-7223.

A name to keep straight: in Pennsylvania, PACE means Pharmaceutical Assistance Contract for the Elderly. Medicare uses the same four letters for a different program.

## Does moving out of Philadelphia change my Medicare plan?

It can. Plans, networks and drug lists are set county by county, and Philadelphia, Delaware, Montgomery and Bucks are separate plan areas. New Jersey across the river is a different state with different plans, so a move across either line means checking your plan.

Plan availability, networks and drug lists are set county by county. Philadelphia County, Delaware County, Montgomery County and Bucks County are separate plan areas. New Jersey, across the river, is a different state with different plans. If you move across one of those lines, see [moving and your Medicare plan](/answers/moved-medicare-new-jersey-new-york) for how the Special Enrollment Period works.

## When can I buy Medigap in Pennsylvania?

During your one-time 6-month Medigap window, which starts when you are 65 or older and have Part B. After it ends, an insurer can turn you down unless you have a guaranteed issue right. Ask PA MEDI what extra protections apply in Pennsylvania.

Under federal law you get a one-time 6-month Medigap Open Enrollment Period that starts when you are 65 or older and have Part B. States can add protections of their own; ask PA MEDI what applies in Pennsylvania before you rely on being able to buy a policy later.

## When can I change Medicare plans in Philadelphia?

The federal dates apply. Open Enrollment runs October 15 to December 7 each year, with changes starting January 1. People already in a Medicare Advantage plan can also switch once between January 1 and March 31. Other windows open after events like a move.

Open Enrollment runs October 15 to December 7 each year. People already in a Medicare Advantage plan can switch once between January 1 and March 31. See [enrollment periods explained](/answers/medicare-enrollment-periods-explained).
