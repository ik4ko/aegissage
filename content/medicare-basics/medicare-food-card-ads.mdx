**The short answer:** there is no universal $900 Medicare food card that everyone on Medicare can claim. Some Medicare Advantage plans do offer supplemental benefits — a grocery or produce allowance, an over-the-counter card — but what exists, who qualifies, and how much it is worth vary by plan, by county, by year, and by your own eligibility. A number in an advertisement is not a benefit you have.

That does not automatically make the ad a scam. It does mean the ad is not the place to find out what you qualify for.

<KeyPoints>

- Original Medicare (Part A and Part B) does not include a grocery or food-card benefit.
- Some **Medicare Advantage** plans offer supplemental benefits. Availability is set county by county and changes every contract year.
- Many of the larger allowances are tied to **additional eligibility rules** — often a qualifying chronic condition, or Medicaid status.
- You should never give your Medicare number to someone who called you.

</KeyPoints>

## What might the ad actually be referring to?

An ad like this can point at several different things. Without the plan name you cannot tell which, and that ambiguity is usually the point. It may involve:

- a **Medicare Advantage supplemental benefit** offered by a specific plan
- an **over-the-counter (OTC) or plan debit-card benefit** for approved items
- a **food or produce allowance** available to members who meet the plan's rules
- **Medicaid or dual-eligible assistance**, if someone qualifies for both Medicare and Medicaid
- a **Special Supplemental Benefit for the Chronically Ill (SSBCI)**, which by design is limited to members with a qualifying condition
- a **lead-generation advertisement** — one designed to get you on the phone with a salesperson, which does not by itself guarantee any benefit

This is not a complete list, and plans differ. The honest position is that nobody can tell you which of these an ad means without knowing the plan name and your county.

## Why the dollar amount can be misleading

A figure in an advertisement is rarely the figure that lands in someone's hands. It may be:

- an **annual or combined** allowance presented as though it were monthly
- a **maximum**, not a guaranteed amount
- redeemable on **eligible items only** — often not a general grocery budget
- restricted to **selected retailers** or a specific card network
- available only in **certain counties**
- available only to **members of one specific plan**
- available only to people meeting **additional eligibility requirements** on top of being enrolled

Any one of those can turn a headline number into something quite different in practice. Several of them together usually do.

<Callout type="watch" title="What the number is not">
A dollar amount in an ad is not a promise made to you. Until you know the plan name, your county, and which eligibility rules apply, the number tells you nothing about what you would actually receive.
</Callout>

## Questions to ask before you give anyone your information

If you are on the phone with someone about one of these offers, these questions cost nothing and settle most of it quickly:

1. **Who is sponsoring this advertisement?** A specific plan, or a marketing company?
2. **What is the exact plan name?**
3. **Is this benefit available in my county?**
4. **Is it available to everyone on the plan, or only to people meeting specific eligibility rules?**
5. **Is the amount monthly, quarterly, annual — or a maximum?**
6. **What products and which retailers actually qualify?**
7. **Does taking this require changing my Medicare coverage?** This is the important one.
8. **What personal information do you genuinely need right now, and why?**

If those questions are met with pressure, vagueness, or urgency, that is your answer.

## Protecting your Medicare information

Do not give your **Medicare Number**, **Social Security number**, **bank or card details**, or **passwords** to someone who contacted you out of the blue — by phone, text, email, or at your door. A legitimate plan representative does not need those things to answer the eight questions above.

Medicare will not call you to sell you a plan. If you are unsure whether a call is genuine, hang up and call **1-800-MEDICARE (1-800-633-4227)** yourself, using the number you looked up rather than one you were given.

You can also check your own plan's benefits directly at [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov), or compare what is genuinely available in your ZIP code with the official [plan finder](https://www.medicare.gov/plan-compare).

## What I saw working in a call center

Before I went independent I worked in a Medicare call center, and I took calls from people responding to advertisements promising large food-card amounts.

The amount was never universal. Depending on the caller's ZIP code, their plan, whether they had Medicaid, and which eligibility rules applied, the same advertised number could mean something substantial, something small, or nothing at all — because plenty of the people calling did not qualify for it.

What stayed with me is that almost nobody calling had been told which of those they were. They had been told a number.

## What to do next

1. **Write down the exact wording of the ad**, and the plan name if it gives one.
2. **Do not call the number in the ad first.** Find out what is actually available in your county before you talk to whoever paid for the placement.
3. **Check your current plan's benefits** — if you are already on a Medicare Advantage plan, you may have an OTC or grocery benefit you are not using.
4. If you want to understand how supplemental benefits fit into the bigger picture, start with [Medicare Advantage or Medigap, compared honestly](/medicare-basics/medicare-advantage-vs-medigap), or [the four parts in plain English](/medicare-basics/the-four-parts-in-plain-english).

Because these benefits are set county by county, where you live decides most of it — see the local notes for [Bergen County](/medicare-bergen-county), [New York City](/medicare-new-york-city) and [Philadelphia](/medicare-philadelphia), or [how enrollment works in each state I am licensed in](/plans).
