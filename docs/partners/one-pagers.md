## 1. Primary care practices and medical groups

**A neutral place to send patients' Medicare questions**

Your staff hear it every week: "Which plan should I pick?" They should not
answer, and most do not want to. I am a licensed insurance broker in
Edgewater who works only on Medicare.

What you do
- Tell patients a licensed broker is available, and give them my card or number. Nothing else.

What the patient does
- Calls me or uses the form on my website, if they choose to.

What I do
- Explain how the parts of Medicare fit together and when changes are allowed.
- Review a patient's options with them only after they agree in writing to that conversation.
- Tell them when staying put is the right answer.
- Work in English, Georgian or Russian.

What does not happen
- No patient names, lists, charts or phone numbers are shared with me.
- No conversations about plans in exam rooms, treatment areas or anywhere care is delivered.
- No payment, gift or meal to the practice or its staff, and none from it.
- No recommendation by your staff of any plan or company.

## 2. Senior living communities and town senior programs

**A plain-English Medicare talk for residents and members**

Forty-five minutes, then questions. No plan is named. Nobody is enrolled.

What the talk covers
- The parts of Medicare and what each pays for.
- Enrollment periods, and the penalties for missing them.
- What a move into a community changes, and the window it opens.
- How to read a plan letter, and how to check everything on Medicare.gov.

How it is run
- I send the outline in advance for your approval.
- No sign-up sheet that collects contact details. My card is on the table; residents who want to talk call me later.
- No plan materials, no enrollment forms, no appointments made in the room.
- Light refreshments only if you provide them. I bring no gifts.

What does not happen
- No resident list or contact information shared with me.
- No fee to the community or program, and none from it.

## 3. Employers and HR teams

**For employees working past 65 and people about to retire**

The questions HR gets: Do I have to sign up at 65? What happens to my HSA?
When does Part B start if I retire in June? Answering them is not HR's job,
and a wrong answer can cost an employee a lifetime penalty.

What I offer
- A group session, in person or by video, on how Medicare timing works with employer coverage.
- A one-page handout your benefits team can give to anyone turning 65.
- One-to-one conversations for employees who ask for one themselves.

Limits
- I do not advise on your group plan, its design or its cost.
- I do not tell employees to leave the group plan.
- No employee names, ages or census data are shared with me.
- No fee to the employer, and none from it.

## 4. Elder law and estate planning firms

**The Medicare part of your client's situation**

Your clients' legal matters often sit beside a Medicare decision: a parent
turning 65, a move across the river, a plan that is ending, a spouse entering
long-term care. I handle the Medicare part and send the client back to you
with a written summary.

How it works
- You tell the client I am available. The client calls me if they wish.
- With the client's permission, I tell you what was decided, so the legal plan and the coverage fit together.
- I refer clients who need a will, a power of attorney or Medicaid planning to an attorney. I do not give legal advice.

Limits
- No referral fees in either direction.
- No client information sent to me by the firm; the client contacts me.
- I do not advise on Medicaid eligibility or asset planning.
