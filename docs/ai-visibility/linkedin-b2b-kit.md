# LinkedIn kit for organizations: 12 posts and 6 messages

For practice managers, billing and revenue cycle leads, growth and community outreach teams at senior-focused medical groups, senior living directors, community program coordinators and HR. Each post links to one `/for-organizations` page. Post two a week for six weeks, or one a week for three months.

## Rules for every post and message

- **Plan-neutral.** No insurance company or plan names, no premiums, no "best", no "you qualify".
- **Nothing of value in either direction.** Never offer or ask for referral fees, gifts or lunch. Say so plainly when it fits; decision-makers worry about it.
- **No lists, ever.** Organizations never send names, schedules or records. People contact you themselves.
- **Plain and calm.** Short sentences. Write for a busy manager, not a marketer.
- **The TPMO sentence** goes on any post that mentions Medicare plans or enrollment (marked *TPMO*). Use the CY2027 wording, with the numbers from the site footer on the day you post:

  > We do not offer every plan available in your area. Currently we represent {organizations} organizations which offer {products} products in Bergen County, NJ. Please contact Medicare.gov or 1-800-MEDICARE to get information on all of your options.

- **Don't tag a client organization** or use its logo without written permission.

**2. Billing leads: January denials** · link: /for-organizations/billing-teams · *TPMO*
Every January, some Medicare claims bounce because a patient's plan changed on January 1. The claim stays with your billing team. The patient can still get help understanding their new coverage, and if they are allowed to change plans, choosing one that fits. That part I can do, at the patient's request, with no patient lists.

**3. Revenue cycle companies** · link: /for-organizations/billing-teams
If your company bills for practices in Bergen or Hudson County, your clients' patients have Medicare questions your team can't answer. A one-page sheet your client practices can hand to those patients might help. Plan-neutral, no fees, no data shared.

**4. Growth teams at senior-focused primary care: education calendars** · link: /for-organizations/medical-groups · *TPMO*
Patient education talks work best when nobody is selling. I give plain Medicare talks in a community room or online: the parts of Medicare, the enrollment windows, how to read a plan letter. No insurance company is named and no applications are taken. Your team approves the outline first.

**5. Growth teams: "keep your doctor"** · link: /for-organizations/medical-groups · *TPMO*
The question your patients ask most at enrollment time is "Can I keep my doctor?" When a patient asks me, I check which plans available to them include their doctors, across the plans I can offer, and I tell them where to see every plan. I don't favor plans your group contracts with. That's what makes it useful.

**6. What changed in 2027 for patient events** · link: /for-organizations/medical-groups
Three Medicare rule changes for 2027 that make patient events simpler: an educational talk needs no Scope of Appointment; one-on-one help can be the same day once the Scope of Appointment is agreed and recorded; and there is no longer a required gap between a talk and one-on-one help. (42 CFR 422.2264, CY2027 final rule.)

**7. New practices: day one** · link: /for-organizations/new-practices
Opening a new practice in North Jersey or New York City? New patients bring Medicare questions in the first week. I provide plain-language guides for the front desk and a short talk for your opening event. Patients contact me themselves. No fees, no placement payments.

**8. New practices: a checklist your patients can use** · link: /for-organizations/new-practices
A small thing new practices can offer: a printable Medicare checklist. A patient's doctors, prescriptions and ZIP code, on one page, before any coverage conversation. It's free and there's no email gate. Happy to drop off printed copies for your waiting room.

**9. Senior living directors: education day** · link: /for-organizations/senior-living · *TPMO*
A Medicare education day for residents and families, with no plan names and no sign-up sheets. Afterward, residents who ask can meet one-on-one the same day, once a Scope of Appointment is agreed and recorded. I visit a resident's room only when the resident invites me. No cost to your community.

**10. Senior living: move-in season** · link: /for-organizations/senior-living · *TPMO*
When a parent moves into senior living, their Medicare plan may not serve the new address, and the move can open a window to change plans. Families usually find out late. A short talk at your family night, or a guide for your admissions team, can head that off.

**11. Community and religious organizations: talks in three languages** · link: /for-organizations/community-programs
I give Medicare education talks in English, Georgian and Russian, for town senior programs, libraries, and community and religious organizations in North Jersey and New York City. 45 minutes plus questions, no plan names, no sign-up sheets, no cost.

**12. HR teams: employees turning 65** · link: /for-organizations/employers · *TPMO*
The most expensive Medicare mistake I see: someone retires, takes COBRA, and assumes it buys time to sign up for Part B. It doesn't. A 30-minute lunch-hour talk for employees approaching 65 can prevent that. HR shares no employee information; people contact me themselves.
